# Stock Market Fundamentals Summary

## 1. What is a Stock Market?

A stock market is a marketplace where shares of publicly listed companies are bought and sold. Companies can raise capital by issuing shares, while investors can buy or sell those shares through recognized stock exchanges. In India, the two major stock exchanges are NSE and BSE.

## 2. NSE and BSE

**NSE (National Stock Exchange):** One of India's major stock exchanges. Its well-known benchmark index is the Nifty 50.

**BSE (BSE Ltd., formerly Bombay Stock Exchange):** India's oldest stock exchange. Its major benchmark index is the Sensex.

## 3. Nifty and Sensex

**Nifty 50:** An index that tracks 50 major companies listed on the NSE across different sectors.

**Sensex:** An index that tracks 30 major companies listed on the BSE.

These indices are commonly used to understand the overall direction of the Indian equity market.

## 4. IPO and SME IPO

**IPO (Initial Public Offering):** The process through which a private company offers its shares to the public for the first time and becomes publicly listed.

**SME IPO:** An IPO specifically designed for eligible small and medium-sized enterprises. SME IPOs are subject to specific listing and regulatory requirements.

## 5. Market Capitalization

Market capitalization represents the total market value of a company's outstanding shares.

**Formula:**

Market Capitalization = Current Share Price × Number of Outstanding Shares

Companies are commonly classified as large-cap, mid-cap, or small-cap based on their market capitalization.

## 6. P/E Ratio

The Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio compares a company's share price with its earnings per share.

**Formula:**

P/E = Market Price per Share ÷ Earnings per Share

It indicates how much investors are paying for each unit of the company's earnings.

## 7. P/B Ratio

The Price-to-Book (P/B) ratio compares a company's market value with its book value.

**Formula:**

P/B = Market Price per Share ÷ Book Value per Share

It is useful when comparing a company's market valuation with the accounting value of its net assets.

## 8. EPS

**Earnings Per Share (EPS)** represents the portion of a company's profit attributable to each outstanding share.

**Formula:**

EPS = Profit Attributable to Equity Shareholders ÷ Number of Outstanding Shares

## 9. Dividend

A dividend is a portion of a company's profits distributed to eligible shareholders. Companies may declare dividends based on their profitability, cash position, and dividend policy.

## 10. Bonus and Stock Split

**Bonus Issue:** A company issues additional shares to existing shareholders without requiring them to pay for those shares, subject to applicable regulations and the company's decision.

**Stock Split:** A company divides existing shares into a larger number of shares, reducing the face value per share proportionally. The split itself does not change the total value of an investor's holding immediately.

## 11. Trading Volume

Trading volume represents the number of shares traded during a particular period. Higher volume generally indicates greater trading activity and liquidity.

## 12. Financial Statements

### Balance Sheet

A balance sheet presents a company's financial position at a particular date. It contains:

* Assets
* Liabilities
* Equity

**Accounting equation:**

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

### Profit & Loss Statement

The Profit & Loss statement shows the company's financial performance over a period. Important items include:

* Revenue
* Operating expenses
* Operating profit
* Finance costs
* Tax
* Net profit

### Cash Flow Statement

The cash flow statement explains how cash moves through the business. It is generally divided into:

* Operating activities
* Investing activities
* Financing activities

## 13. Basic Financial Ratios

Financial ratios help analysts evaluate profitability, efficiency, liquidity, and financial structure.

Common ratios include:

* **Profit Margin** = Net Profit ÷ Revenue × 100
* **Operating Margin** = Operating Profit ÷ Revenue × 100
* **Debt-to-Equity** = Total Debt ÷ Shareholders' Equity
* **ROE** = Net Profit ÷ Shareholders' Equity × 100
* **Current Ratio** = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities

## Conclusion

Understanding stock market concepts and financial statements helps a Data Analyst interpret financial datasets, identify business trends, calculate meaningful metrics, and communicate insights to business and technology teams. This knowledge is particularly useful in a FinTech environment where financial and market data are frequently analyzed.
